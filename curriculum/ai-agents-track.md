# AI & Agents Track — 5-Day Residency Syllabus

*Jungli AI Learning Residency · Dandeli, Karnataka · Lead instructor: Alan Oliver*

## The track in one paragraph

Five days to go from **zero to building real software with AI**. You don't need
to be a coder. You'll learn to drive **Claude Code** — an AI that builds, edits,
and ships software for you — and use it to put a real website live on the
internet, build a working app, and start your own project that matters to you.
The goal isn't to memorise syntax. It's to leave genuinely able to turn "I wish
something existed" into a thing that exists, on your own, after you go home.

## Who this is for

- People who use AI casually (ChatGPT/Claude for prompts and content) and want
  to actually **build** things, not just chat.
- Founders, professionals, creators, freelancers — anyone with ideas they'd love
  to turn into a website, tool, or app.
- **No coding experience required.** If you've never opened a terminal, you're
  exactly who this is designed for.

**Not for:** experienced engineers wanting advanced agent architecture. (That's
a different, more advanced track — this one starts from the ground up.)

## What you'll leave with

1. **A live landing page** with a working email-capture form — your offer,
   audience page, or portfolio.
2. **A "second brain"** — your CV, notes, or product info turned into context an
   AI can actually use.
3. **A chat agent grounded in your docs** — a recruiter bot for your portfolio,
   or a customer bot for your product.
4. **Your own project** — something genuinely useful for your work or life,
   scoped and built with the instructor mentoring you 1:1.
5. **The foundation that matters most:** real confidence driving Claude Code —
   briefing it, planning with it, debugging it, saving your work with git, and
   shipping. You won't need this course to keep building afterwards.

## The shape of the week

| Day | When | Theme | You leave with |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | Mon morning | **Zero → a live site** | A live landing page with email capture, on a real URL |
| **2** | Tue morning | **Driving the agent + your second brain** | A second brain the agent can use + the core skills: brief, plan, debug, save |
| **3** | Wed morning | **Building a chat agent** | A chat grounded in your docs + your own project scoped |

The week is a deliberate arc: **build confidence Mon–Wed, then apply it**
Thursday in self-directed time — all three tracks running together, instructors
available across the room — and **land the plane Friday morning**.

## Day 1 — Zero → a live website *(Monday morning)*

**Goal:** Everyone ends **the morning** with a website live on the internet. No
exceptions. This is the confidence win the whole week is built on — and because
the afternoon belongs to another track, we make it land inside the session.

**We'll teach (short, ~30%):**
- What an LLM and an "AI agent" actually are, without the mysticism.
- What Claude Code is and how it thinks: it reads and writes files on your
  computer, runs commands, makes a plan, tries things, and sometimes fails — and
  that's normal.
- The core loop you'll use all week: **ask → it builds → you look → you change it.**
- What git and GitHub are, gently: "save points" for your work, and the way to
  put it on the internet.

**We'll build (the rest of the morning):**
- Use Claude Code to build a **landing page** for an offer (your portfolio, a
  product, a waitlist) — starting from a ready, prompt-driven template.
- Add a real **email-signup form** (a drop-in Kit embed — no API keys needed).
- Save it to GitHub and deploy it to **Vercel** to get a live URL.
- Text the link to a friend. That's the moment.

**Leave with:** a live landing page with email capture + the realisation that you
can make the internet do things.

## Day 2 — Driving the agent + your second brain *(Tuesday morning)*

**Goal:** Turn yesterday's "it worked!" into real skill — and build a **second
brain**: a place the agent stores and reads what it knows about you (or your
product). This is how you'll *feel* context and memory, the thing that makes
agents actually work.

**We'll teach:**
- How to **brief** Claude Code properly — being specific, giving it context,
  asking it to plan before it builds.
- **Context & memory:** what the agent can and can't "see," keeping it focused,
  and giving it standing instructions it reads every time.
- How to **debug calmly** when it gets stuck or does something daft (a skill, not
  a panic).
- **git as a safety net:** commit when things work, go back when they break.

**We'll build:**
- A **second brain** — turn your CV/notes (or your product info) into tidy, linked
  notes the agent can search and answer from.
- Ask it real questions and watch it answer *only* from what you gave it.

**Leave with:** your own second brain + the four core skills (brief, plan, debug,
save) — and a real feel for how agents use context.

## Day 3 — Building a chat agent *(Saturday — full day)*

**Goal:** Build a real app that *does* something genuinely useful: a **chat that
answers questions from your documents.** A full weekend day with the instructor
present — we build it together in the morning, then you start scoping your own
project for tomorrow.

**We'll teach:**
- What an app is actually made of, in plain terms (the page you see vs. the bit
  that does the work behind it).
- How to connect to an **AI model** and keep it honest — answering only from the
  context you give it.
- **API keys & secrets:** what they are and how to keep them safe.

**We'll build (together, same app for everyone):**
- A one-page **chat grounded in a sample person's CV** — ask it questions, and
  paste a job description to get a **fit assessment**.
- Deploy it live, like Day 1.
- (The same build works for a business: a bot that answers customers' questions
  about your product.)

**Leave with:** a working, deployed chat agent — and the skills to point it at
*your own* career or product tomorrow.

## Day 4 — Your project: 0 → 1 *(Sunday — full day)*

**Goal:** Build something *you* actually want. This is the heart of the week — a
full weekend day with the instructor as your mentor, which is exactly why the
heavy build lives here rather than a rushed weekday slot.

**How the day works:**
- **Scoping clinic (the most important skill):** each person picks a real use
  case from their work or life, and we cut it down to something buildable in a
  day. Scoping well is 80% of success — the instructor works through this with
  each person.
- **Build time:** you build your project with Claude Code; the instructor floats
  to unblock, debug, and make decisions with you.
- **Patterns clinic:** mid-afternoon, we pause to look at the failures that came
  up across the room — better prompts, when to start a piece over, common traps.

**Leave with:** your own real project, scoped and substantially built.

## Day 5 — Ship, demo, keep going *(Monday morning)*

**Goal:** Finish, share, and make sure you can keep doing this without us — a
punchy final morning to land the plane before send-off.

**How the day works:**
- **Polish & ship:** finish your project and deploy it live.
- **Demo circle:** everyone shows what they built — what it does, who it's for,
  what's still rough, what's next. Short and supportive.
- **Peer testing:** try each other's projects; it's the fastest way to see what's
  actually usable.
- **Keep-going session:** how to keep building after the jungle — where to get
  unstuck, how to manage your Claude usage and cost sensibly, and what to build
  next.

**Leave with:** a polished, demoed project and a concrete plan to keep building.

## How the week is scheduled

This is a **blended three-track residency**, and this track runs on a
**mornings-plus-weekend** rhythm built around the instructor's availability:

- **Weekday mornings = AI & Agents (this track).** A ~3-hour session that ends
  with something *finished* — because the afternoon belongs to another track,
  each morning has to stand on its own.
- **Weekday afternoons = the other tracks** (Creative and Automation), run by the
  other instructors. Your day stays full and you learn across all three tracks.
- **Lunch & dinner = grab-the-instructor windows.** Bring blockers from the
  morning; an hour or two of unblocking over food.
- **The weekend = the deep build.** Two full days with the instructor present,
  where you build your own real project 0→1 with proper 1:1 mentoring.

**A weekday morning:**

| Time | Block |
|---|---|
| 09:30–10:15 | Lesson — the day's concept, with live demos |
| 10:15–12:15 | Guided build — apply it immediately; instructor moves around helping |
| 12:15–12:30 | Wrap — what you shipped, what to chew on, blockers to raise at lunch |

**A weekend day** is the inverse: mostly hands-on building on *your own* project,
with a short kick-off, a midday patterns clinic, floating 1:1 mentoring, and demos
near the end.

Roughly **30% teaching / 70% building** across the week. Mornings are heavily
scaffolded and paired (many learners have never used a terminal); by the Sunday
deep-build, people are driving their own projects with the instructor mentoring.

## How we work (built around making, not listening)

- **A concrete thing to ship every day** — small enough to finish, useful enough
  to care about.
- **Bring-your-own-project** from Day 4 — adapt the skills to your real business,
  audience, or idea so it doesn't become another folder of dead tutorial code.
- **Debugging clinics** — we take real failures from the room and fix them
  together. Broken builds are the curriculum, not an interruption.
- **Peer testing & demo circles** — short daily demos build the habit of showing
  unfinished work; the Day 5 showcase is the polished version.
- **Shared build log** — everyone notes what they built, what broke, what they
  learnt. By the end the group has a real library of fixes and patterns.

## Facilitator notes (Alan)

- **Protect the Day 1 win.** If anyone leaves Day 1 without a live URL, the week
  gets harder. Pre-arrival setup (see `prep/`) exists so Day 1 isn't lost to
  installs.
- **Scoping is the lesson on Day 4**, not the build itself. Most failures are
  over-scoped ideas, not bad code.
- **Normalise failure early and often.** The "not technical enough" fear is real
  for ~1 in 3 learners (per the survey) — name it on Day 1.
- **Watch Claude usage limits** during full-day building; see `prep/claude-plans.md`
  for the plan guidance and the fallback for anyone who runs low.
- **Mornings must be self-contained.** Afternoons belong to the other tracks, so
  there's no spill-over time — each weekday morning ends with a finished result.
  Extended building happens on the weekend.
- **Coordinate the weekend with your colleagues.** This track claims both full
  weekend days for project builds — confirm the Creative/Automation tracks aren't
  relying on weekend time. (To settle at the team meeting, along with the exact
  Thu→Mon dates.)

## After the residency

You leave with working projects, a clear mental model for building with AI, and —
most importantly — the ability to keep going on your own. The personal site and
your own project are yours to keep developing. The skill is yours for good.
