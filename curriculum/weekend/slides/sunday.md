# 01
## Give it a memory

---

<!-- header: 'Sunday · Make it yours & grow it' -->

## Yesterday → today

- **Yesterday:** you built an app and **owned the code**.
- **Today:** own the *whole thing*, **add a feature**, and **ship it**.

Deeper than Saturday — and that's fine. Claude Code does the heavy lifting; you drive.

---

## Context is everything

- An AI only knows **what's in front of it**.
- **Context** = what you give it to work from.
- A **`CLAUDE.md`** is your app's standing memory — read *every* time.

Get good at context and you can build almost anything.

---

<!-- _class: build -->

## Give your app a memory

```text
Write a CLAUDE.md for this project: what it is, its stack,
how to run it, key files, and rules for working in it.
Keep it short and clear.
```

Read it. From now on, every session starts already **knowing your app**.

# 02
## Own the backend & grow it

---

## Own the backend too

- Yesterday you owned the **code**. The **database** may still be the builder's.
- Owning your app means owning the **backend** too — move it to *your* account.
- Your app carries its own **recipe** (a migrations folder) — it's a rebuild, not a rewrite.

*(Instructor demos this — the deepest part of the weekend.)*

---

<!-- _class: build -->

## Grow it: email me when someone books

```text
When someone submits the booking form, email me the details.
Use Resend and a server-side function; keep the API key as a
server-side secret, never in the browser or git. One step at a time.
```

A real feature — the one the builder wanted to charge you for.

---

<!-- _class: trap -->

## Real secrets live server-side

- A real API key **never** goes in the app's `.env` or the browser.
- It lives **server-side**, as a secret.
- Same habit as Saturday — one level up.

*And first emails often hit **Spam** — mark "Not spam," it learns.*

# 03
## Debug & ship

---

<!-- _class: trap -->

## When it's quiet, get the log

- Sometimes everything looks right and **nothing happens**.
- **Don't guess. Get the log.** Every step leaves a trail.
- The log turns a mystery into a **one-line fix**.

Reaching for the log instead of poking around — that's a real developer skill.

---

<!-- _class: build -->

## Deploy it as yours

```text
Deploy this app to Vercel on my account: connect my GitHub
repo, set the environment variables, and give me a live URL.
Tell me what to click.
```

Your own URL — and it **redeploys itself** on every change.
